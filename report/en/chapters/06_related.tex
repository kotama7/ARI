\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Self-improvement and co-evolution}\label{sec:related:treesearch}

Reflection and self-refinement methods feed model-generated critiques back
into a later attempt \cite{madaan2023self,shinn2023reflexion}. They improve
an artefact or trajectory while leaving the evaluator and authority
structure largely fixed. Constitutional ARI-RQGM instead treats prompt
roles and utility policy as versioned institutional artefacts whose
replacement requires evidence, role separation, and a legal transition.

The closest conceptual ancestor is the Red Queen G\"odel Machine
\cite{iacob2026red}, where agents and evaluators co-evolve and the scoring
function can change. ARI-RQGM adopts co-evolution and adversarial replay,
but adds a fixed constitution, epoch transactions, single-writer
registries, target-bound sanctions, clean-room replacement, and explicit
separation between mutable policy and immutable verification. We do not
claim the co-evolution or boundary updates already present in RQGM as new.
The conceptual delta is to enclose the mutable institution in a capability
matrix and complete component-lifecycle table, then connect retirement repair and
fixed evidence checking on the same provenance graph. The systems
contribution is this composition and its inspectable boundary rather than
the individual mechanisms.

Constitutional AI uses written principles for self-critique and AI
feedback \cite{bai2022constitutional}. Our constitution is different in
kind: it fixes a capability matrix, state transitions, and evidence
formats that model outputs cannot waive. AI Control evaluates protocols
that separate trusted and untrusted models under attempted subversion
\cite{greenblatt2023ai}. Our scope is narrower, and we have not yet
performed a quantitative collusion-control evaluation; we nevertheless
avoid placing writing, review, adjudication, and registry mutation under
one authority.

\subsection{Autonomous research systems}\label{sec:related:autonomous}

The AI Scientist demonstrated an end-to-end loop from ideation through
experimentation and manuscript preparation \cite{lu2024ai}; later work
analysed the broader trajectory toward automated scientific discovery
\cite{lu2026towards}. AIDE treats experimental code improvement as an
iterative agent problem \cite{jiang2025aide}. These systems establish the
value of persistent artefacts, executable evaluation, and automated
writing. Their primary object of optimisation is the research artefact.
The object of Constitutional ARI-RQGM is the institution that proposes,
evaluates, and writes those artefacts.

Agent Laboratory explores coordinated model roles as research assistants
\cite{schmidgall2025agent}, while MLE-bench provides executable tasks for
machine-learning engineering agents \cite{chan2024mle}. The constitutional
boundary is agnostic to whether an underlying actor uses prompted
reasoning \cite{wei2022chain} or large-scale candidate generation
\cite{li2022competition}; it governs the actor's version, authority, and
evidence rather than its internal generation procedure.

Story2Proposal argues for contracts that connect narrative scientific
claims to executable proposals and evidence \cite{qian2026story2proposal}.
The governed paper archive uses the same general insight as an immutable
writer anchor: an evolving reviewer cannot make an execution-grounded
falsehood true.

\subsection{Evaluation and evidence}\label{sec:related:eval}

PaperBench evaluates whether agents can reproduce research papers through
a structured rubric \cite{starace2025paperbench}. Its central lesson for
governance is that an evaluation claim needs an explicit task structure,
observable artefacts, and a grading boundary. Constitutional ARI-RQGM
extends that lesson from final outcomes to institutional history: attacks,
defences, judgments, transitions, and repairs are typed records whose
provenance is retained.

Adversarial evaluation is often used to harden a fixed model or policy.
Here validated attacks have two additional functions. They may support
accountability for a serving component, and they become replay cases for
candidate successors. The same failure is therefore both evidence about
the incumbent and an entrance test for its replacement.

\subsection{Position of this work}\label{sec:related:foundations}

Least privilege, complete mediation, and separation of privilege are
classical protection-design principles \cite{saltzer1975protection}.
Deterministic state-machine replication grounds recovery of the same
state from the same committed input sequence
\cite{schneider1990implementing}. Recent event-sourced agent designs
similarly use append-only events and deterministic projections
\cite{nakajima2026the}. We therefore do not claim single-writer state,
replay, or append-only history as inventions. The technical task is to
connect them consistently to RQGM epochs, attribution, retirement repair,
and manuscript evidence checking.

The \href{https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/}{W3C PROV-O Recommendation}
standardises interoperable entity--activity--agent provenance.
\href{https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/torres-arias}{in-toto}
uses signed supply-chain step evidence, and
\href{https://docs.sigstore.dev/logging/overview/}{Sigstore Rekor}
provides a publicly auditable append-only transparency log. Those systems
offer provenance interoperability or cryptographic/witnessed integrity that
our local unsigned checkpoint does not. Conversely, they do not define the
RQGM-specific retirement closure or utility re-scoring used here.

Transparent logs and software-supply-chain provenance ask which bytes and
identity were committed; reference monitors and capability systems ask
which subject may perform which operation on which object; security
automata and state-machine methods ask whether every execution trace obeys
a safety language \cite{schneider2000enforceable}. These are relevant lineages rather than new claims of
this paper. The present implementation borrows least privilege and complete
mediation \cite{saltzer1975protection} and deterministic state projection
\cite{schneider1990implementing}, but it has neither independently witnessed
log heads nor OS-enforced role capabilities, and its composed epoch,
transaction, quota, and repair state has not been model checked.

Constitutional ARI-RQGM is neither a claim of autonomous scientific
superiority nor a general solution to self-modifying agents. It is a
systems architecture for a narrower question: how can model-defined
research roles and their utility policy change during a run without
letting those roles acquire the authority to ratify their own changes?
Its answer combines co-evolution with deterministic constitutional
enforcement, transactional state, provenance-aware repair, and a fixed
evidence gate. The contribution is the composition of these mechanisms
and the explicit boundary between what they guarantee and what still
requires empirical study.
