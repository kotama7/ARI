\begin{abstract}
Autonomous research systems increasingly delegate proposal, evaluation,
and writing decisions to model-defined roles. If those roles and their
scoring policy can evolve, the system may improve its institution as well
as its artefacts; it may also learn to reward itself, conceal failures, or
expand its own authority. We present \textbf{Constitutional ARI-RQGM}, a
governed co-evolution architecture that separates an immutable
deterministic kernel from evolvable prompts, components, and utility
policies. Institutional changes commit only at epoch boundaries; T16
emergency quarantine forces an immediate boundary with a new fingerprint. At a boundary,
adversarial evidence, role-separated prosecution and defence, replay and
anchor evaluation, and a fixed T1--T21 transition table determine which
candidates may enter service. Registry writes are single-authority,
transactions are replayable, and retirement triggers provenance-aware
frontier repair without deleting history. The same constitution governs a
paper archive in which writer and reviewer roles co-evolve while a fixed,
structured numeric claim--evidence gate remains outside their authority.
The complete transition table, capability matrix, event contract, recovery
procedure, and determinism boundary are specified. All 1,124 directly
relevant tests pass, and five fixed fault fixtures are rejected while one
clean fixture passes. These results do not establish improved research
quality, general detection rates, attribution accuracy, or cost benefit.
\end{abstract}
